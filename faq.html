Questions and answers

What is Veterans of Color?

Veterans of Color is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit organization building a comprehensive shelter and transitional housing program for female veterans experiencing homelessness or housing instability in Fresno, California. We combine safe housing with wraparound services — health and trauma-informed care, employment and education support, VA benefits help, family support, and peer community — so women veterans can transition to stable, independent living.

Who can stay at the shelter?

Our program is designed for female veterans ages 25–55 who are experiencing homelessness, with priority for women in immediate housing crisis or at risk of losing housing. We serve Fresno and California's Central Valley, with an emphasis on women of color. All services are free to residents.

When does the shelter open?

We are live as an organization and currently in our building stages — actively securing our 30-bed Fresno facility, assembling our team, and formalizing partnerships with the VA and community providers. Our grand opening will be announced here and to our supporters. Join our contact list to be notified, or make a founding pledge to help us open sooner.

Why housing specifically for women veterans?

Women are the fastest-growing segment of the veteran homeless population, and female veterans become homeless at two to four times the rate of civilian women. Yet gender-specific veteran housing remains critically scarce. Women veterans face distinct challenges — including high rates of military sexual trauma, single parenthood, and the need for safe, women-centered environments — that mixed-gender programs often aren't designed to address. A home built for women veterans, by people who understand their experience, produces better outcomes.

What is MST, and do you help survivors?

MST — military sexual trauma — is the term the VA and Department of Defense use for sexual assault or harassment experienced during military service. Research cited in our planning shows more than half of homeless female veterans have experienced MST. Yes: trauma-informed PTSD and MST care is a core part of our Health & Wellness program, delivered by trained, culturally competent counselors in a safe, women-only environment.

Is there a cost for residents?

No. Every service at Veterans of Color — housing, counseling, job training, benefits help, childcare, and community programs — is completely free to the women we serve. As a nonprofit, we are funded by government and foundation grants, corporate sponsors, and individual donors.

How will my donation be used?

Right now, founding donations go directly toward securing and preparing our Fresno facility and launching services — the beds, the counseling rooms, the staff who will welcome our first residents. Our Year-One operating plan directs the majority of funding to personnel and programs. As a 501(c)(3) nonprofit, your gift is tax-deductible to the extent allowed by law, and we are committed to transparent reporting on outcomes.

How can I volunteer or partner with you?

We'd love to have you. Volunteers help with facility preparation, mentorship, professional services (legal, medical, trades, tech, marketing), childcare, and events — tell us your interests here. Employers, landlords, healthcare providers, and service organizations can join our partner network — learn more about partnering or contact us to start the conversation.

I'm a veteran in crisis right now. What should I do?

Please don't wait for our doors to open — help is available right now, 24/7, free and confidential: call or text the Veterans Crisis Line at 988, then press 1 (or text 838255). For housing help specifically, call the National Call Center for Homeless Veterans at 1-877-4AID-VET. See our Get Help page for more resources.
